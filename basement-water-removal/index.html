Few problems rattle a homeowner like finding the basement under water. Heavy rain that overloads the drainage, a dead sump pump, a failed water heater, a broken pipe and foundation seepage all put water downstairs, where it has to be dealt with by professionals right away before it damages the structure, grows mold and destroys what you store there. Chesapeake Restoration provides 24/7 emergency basement water removal throughout Chesapeake, using powerful extraction equipment and advanced drying technology to get your basement dry and safe as quickly as possible.

Drying a basement is its own challenge: the space is below ground level, air barely circulates and dampness lingers. Moisture gets trapped by the surrounding soil, the poured floor and the block walls, so it will not evaporate naturally. Our restoration technicians understand these challenges and bring specialized equipment: submersible pumps for deep water, truck-mounted extraction units for large volumes, and dehumidifiers and air movers positioned for below-grade drying.

Beyond removing the water, we address the root cause of your basement flooding to help keep it from happening again. Our technicians inspect the sump pump system, foundation drainage, window wells, wall penetrations and grading to identify what contributed. From there you get our recommendations for lasting fixes, which may include waterproofing, interior drainage or installing a backup sump pump to guard the basement against the next intrusion.

Your Basement Water Removal Questions Answered

It depends on where the water came from. Water from a burst supply pipe or a water heater starts out clean, which is Category 1. Assume contamination when the water backed up through drains, overflowed the sump pit or came through the foundation from outside soil. Clean water also degrades the longer it sits, so we assess the category before deciding what can be saved.
Carpet can often be saved if the water is clean (Category 1) and drying begins within 24 to 48 hours. However, carpet padding almost always requires replacement. If sewage or other contaminated water touched it, which is Category 2 or 3, the carpet has to be thrown out to protect your health.
Plan on 4 to 8 hours for the extraction itself. Full drying tends to run 4 to 7 days in a basement, where moisture-retaining concrete and the underground setting slow everything down. Our crew checks moisture each day and pulls the dryers only once the readings show the structure is properly dry.
Do not step into the water, above all when it has reached appliances or outlets. Power to the lower level should be cut, provided the electrical panel sits somewhere dry. Stop the source if you safely can, for example by closing the main water valve for a burst pipe. Finally, photograph the basement from a dry spot so your insurer can see it.
Expect a typical range of $1,000 to $5,000 for removing water from a Chesapeake basement, with the final figure set by damage, contamination and water volume. A small seepage problem might be $500 to $1,500, and full flooding can reach $3,000 to $8,000 or higher. Sudden water damage is covered under many homeowner's insurance policies, depending on the cause.

The Benefits of Professional Basement Water Removal

Basement water removal takes specialized equipment and expertise beyond what consumer pumps and fans can accomplish. Rental units are far slower than professional submersible pumps, and only commercial drying equipment sets the airflow, temperature and humidity required to dry a space below ground, where natural evaporation barely happens. When drying is not done properly, residual water in the framing, the insulation and the concrete ends in mold and material decay.

A basement under water is hazardous in ways that are easy to overlook. Electrocution is possible whenever water reaches or covers outlets, wiring, the panel or plugged-in appliances. Flooding can also compromise the furnace, the water heater and natural gas lines. Our technicians are trained to assess and mitigate these hazards before extraction begins, keeping your family and our crew safe throughout the restoration.

How to Tell You Need Basement Water Removal

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A damp, mildew smell that meets you on the basement stairs
  • Damp or wet walls, especially along the lower part of the foundation
  • Stained or deteriorating carpet, tile or other basement flooring
  • Any depth of water sitting across the basement slab
  • Leaks at the window frames below grade, or wells holding water outside them
  • Metal posts, water heater feet or furnace cabinets rusting near the floor
  • Sump pump trouble: it never shuts off, never kicks on or sounds wrong

How to Prevent Basement Water Removal Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Make sure downspouts discharge at least 6 feet away from your foundation
  • A water alarm set beside the sump and the water heater warns you before a small leak spreads
  • Clean your gutters so they do not overflow right beside the foundation
  • Four times a year, check that the sump pump works by filling the pit with water
  • Add battery backup to your sump system so it keeps working when the power fails
  • Keep window wells clear of debris and consider adding window well covers
