Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Chesapeake

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. What matters most is what was in it. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

In Chesapeake, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Stop FlushingHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterOld towels can stop clean water before it gets to a hallway or carpet. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
  • Water escaping at the base with every flush because the wax ring has worn out.
  • Bowl water pushed over the rim by a clog in the drain line or the trap.
  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Bacteria in Building Materials

Water from the drain or bowl deposits bacteria in drywall, baseboards, carpet and pad. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Baseboards and door casings sit right at floor level and soak up water from the bottom edge. Most are made of fiberboard, which swells, splits and does not go back to its original shape.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Odor That Returns

When contaminated water dries under flooring or in the base of a wall, the smell comes back in humid weather and stays until that material is removed.
